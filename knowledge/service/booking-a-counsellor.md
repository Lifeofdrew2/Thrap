## How booking works

A person can request a session through the `[ORG-APPROVED CHANNEL]`. The request goes to
`[NAMED HUMAN ROLE]`, who matches the person with a counsellor from the approved panel
and confirms a time directly with them.

A request is not the same as a confirmed appointment. Confirmation comes from the
booking route, not from this navigation service.

## What to expect in a first session

A first session is usually a conversation about what brought the person in, what they
would like to be different, and what kind of support would fit. It is not a test or an
assessment to pass. Nothing has to be prepared in advance, and a person can say that
they do not want to discuss something.

The counsellor explains confidentiality and its limits at the start, and agrees a plan
for how many sessions may help.

## Session format and access

Sessions are offered `[APPROVED FORMATS: in person, telephone, video]` during
`[APPROVED HOURS]`. Languages available on the panel are `[APPROVED LANGUAGES]`.
Accessibility adjustments, including `[APPROVED ADJUSTMENTS]`, can be requested when
booking.

## Typical waiting time

Standard requests are usually offered an appointment within `[APPROVED WAITING TIME]`.
Where a person cannot wait that long, the booking route escalates to
`[NAMED HUMAN ROLE]` rather than leaving the request in a queue.

## Cancelling or changing an appointment

Changes go through the same booking route. Cancelling does not use up an entitlement
where notice of `[APPROVED NOTICE PERIOD]` is given.

## If booking is not the right step yet

Some people want to think about it first. That is a reasonable choice. The programme
stays available, and a person can ask for general information about the service without
committing to a session.
