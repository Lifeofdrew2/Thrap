## The confidentiality principle

What a person discusses with a counsellor is confidential between that person and the
counsellor. The employer is not told who used the service, what was discussed, or what
was decided. Counsellors work to the confidentiality standards of their professional
body and to the organisation's approved confidentiality policy.

## Limits of confidentiality

Confidentiality is not absolute. A counsellor may need to pass information on without
consent where there is a serious risk to the life or safety of the person or someone
else, where a child or vulnerable adult may be at risk of harm, or where disclosure is
required by law or a court order.

Where a counsellor has to act on one of these limits, professional practice is to tell
the person what is being shared and with whom, wherever it is safe to do so.

## What the organisation sees

The organisation receives aggregate, non-identifying information only: for example how
many sessions took place in a period and the broad categories of need. It does not
receive names, session notes, or anything that identifies an individual.

## What this navigation service records

Anonymous navigation is not stored as identifiable conversation content. Conversation
text is held in the browser's active memory for the length of the session and is not
written to browser storage, the page URL, analytics, or logs.

Identified processing happens only after a separate, explicit consent choice, for the
single purpose named in that consent notice. Refusing it leaves anonymous navigation
fully available.

Escalation records are kept separately from conversation content. They hold the minimum
operational facts needed to show that a request was routed and a notification attempted,
and are retained on the schedule at `[APPROVED RETENTION PERIOD]`.
