## What people describe

People describe anxiety as a mind that will not slow down, a sense of dread without an
obvious cause, or being braced for something to go wrong. It is common for the worry to
attach itself to whatever is nearest — a deadline, a message left unread, a
conversation replayed afterwards.

Anxiety is not a character flaw or a failure of willpower. It is a common and treatable
difficulty, and it is one of the most frequent reasons people use workplace counselling.

## How it can show up in the body

Anxiety often has a physical side: a racing heart, tight chest, shallow breathing,
stomach upset, restlessness, muscle tension, or tiredness that sleep does not fix. People
sometimes seek help for the physical symptoms first without connecting them to worry.

Physical symptoms should be checked with a medical professional rather than assumed to
be anxiety. This service cannot rule anything out.

## How it affects daily life

The everyday impact is often what makes people reach out: avoiding meetings or calls,
putting off decisions, checking work repeatedly, difficulty concentrating, irritability
with people at home, or withdrawing from things that used to be enjoyable.

## Signs that professional support would help

Support from a counsellor is likely to help when worry has been present most days for
several weeks or more, when it is interfering with sleep, work, or relationships, when
avoidance is growing, or when a person is carrying it alone.

There is no threshold of severity a person has to reach before they are allowed to ask
for help. Wanting to talk to someone is a sufficient reason.

## What counselling for anxiety involves

Counsellors work with anxiety in structured, evidence-based ways, looking at the
patterns that keep worry going and building more workable responses to it. What that
looks like in practice is decided between the person and their counsellor.

This navigation service does not deliver those approaches, assess severity, or suggest a
diagnosis. Its role is to help a person reach the counsellor who can.
