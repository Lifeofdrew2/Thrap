## What burnout is

Burnout is a syndrome resulting from workplace stress that has not been successfully
managed over a long period. It is usually described in three parts: exhaustion that rest
does not resolve, growing cynicism or distance from the job, and a sense of reduced
effectiveness at work.

Burnout is an occupational phenomenon rather than a medical diagnosis, and it is
specifically about the relationship between a person and their work.

## How it differs from ordinary tiredness

Ordinary tiredness improves with a weekend or a break. Burnout typically does not — people
return from leave already depleted. Another common marker is a shift in feeling towards
work that used to be meaningful, which people often experience as guilt or as evidence
that something is wrong with them.

## Common contributing conditions

Burnout is strongly associated with sustained workload, little control over how work is
done, insufficient recognition, unfair treatment, conflict with colleagues, unclear
expectations, and a mismatch between personal values and the job. These are conditions,
not personal weaknesses, and several of them are outside an individual's control.

## Where it overlaps with other difficulties

Burnout, anxiety, and low mood overlap and often occur together, and sleep problems are
frequently part of the picture. Distinguishing between them is work for a counsellor,
not for this service.

## Signs that professional support would help

Support is likely to help when exhaustion persists after rest, when a person dreads the
working day most days, when performance or relationships are being affected, or when
they are relying on ways of coping that are causing their own problems.
