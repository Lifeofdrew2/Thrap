## What people describe

Sleep difficulty takes several forms: trouble falling asleep, waking repeatedly, waking
early and being unable to return to sleep, or sleeping a full night and still waking
unrefreshed. People often describe lying awake with a mind that becomes busiest at the
moment the lights go out.

## The two-way relationship with stress

Sleep and mental wellbeing affect each other in both directions. Worry and low mood
disrupt sleep, and poor sleep in turn lowers mood, shortens patience, and makes
concentration and decisions harder. This loop is one reason sleep is often the first
thing people mention when they reach out.

## Impact on work and safety

Sustained poor sleep affects attention, judgement, reaction time, and emotional
regulation. Where a role involves driving, operating equipment, or safety-critical
decisions, that impact should be raised through `[APPROVED WORKPLACE ROUTE]` as a safety
matter as well as a wellbeing one.

## When to involve a medical professional

Some sleep problems have physical causes, including breathing-related conditions,
chronic pain, and side effects of existing treatment. Loud snoring with pauses in
breathing, sleepiness that is dangerous during the day, or sleep problems alongside
unexplained physical symptoms should be discussed with a medical professional.

This service does not assess, diagnose, or advise on treatment for any of these, and it
does not discuss medication or sleep aids.

## Signs that professional support would help

Support is likely to help when sleep has been disturbed most nights for several weeks,
when the cause appears to be worry or low mood, or when daytime functioning is
noticeably affected. A counsellor can work with the stress and worry that keep the cycle
going.
