## Common hesitations

People frequently delay contacting a support service for reasons that are worth naming
plainly, because the answers are concrete rather than reassuring generalities.

"Other people have it worse." Support is not rationed by comparison, and there is no
severity threshold a person must reach to be entitled to a conversation.

"My employer will find out." The programme is confidential and the organisation receives
only aggregate, non-identifying information. The limits to that confidentiality are set
out in the confidentiality policy.

"It will affect my job." Use of the programme is separate from performance management
and is not recorded in a personnel file.

"I should be able to handle this myself." Difficulty is not evidence of weakness, and
handling something alone is not a more legitimate outcome than being supported.

"I do not know what I would even say." Nothing needs to be prepared. Starting with the
fact that something is wrong is enough for a first session.

## The cost of waiting

Difficulties that are addressed early usually need less support than the same
difficulties addressed once they have affected sleep, work, and relationships. Waiting
is understandable and very common, and it is not a failure — but earlier contact tends
to mean shorter, easier support.
